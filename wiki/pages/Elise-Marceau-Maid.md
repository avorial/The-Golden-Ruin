# Élise Marceau, Maid

<div class="wiki-layout">
<div class="wiki-main">

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>At The Table</h2>
<p>Élise Marceau moves through the North Star with a tray balanced on one hand and a dozen conclusions already formed. She is young enough that patrons talk over her and sharp enough to use that mistake as an education.</p>

<p>She smiles easily, but never vaguely. If she is being pleasant, it is because she has chosen to be pleasant, not because she has failed to notice the blood on a cuff or the folded letter under a pillow.</p>
</section>

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>Place In The House</h2>
<p>Élise tends bedrooms, linens, fires, lamps, and the long gallery where students linger when they do not want to be alone but cannot bear company. She knows who has nightmares, who writes home, who destroys letters before sending them, and who hides drawings face-down beneath the mattress.</p>

<p>She is often the first member of staff to understand when a student is truly afraid.</p>
</section>

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>Public Face</h2>
<p>To guests, Élise is quick, polite, and almost invisible. To students, she is more approachable than Burke and less intimidating than Madame Voclain.</p>

<p>She can be bribed with gossip, kindness, or a sincere apology. Money alone rarely works. Too many wealthy people have tried it badly.</p>
</section>

## Hidden Truth

Élise is not a Caretaker, though Burke has deliberately kept her close because she sees patterns before others do. She has noticed that certain students draw the North Star before they know its story, and that those drawings often appear after a night when the main courtyard sounds wrong.

She is the staff member most likely to help the characters without demanding a full explanation.

## What Élise Is Hiding

Élise has found a folded page hidden behind a loose skirting board in one of the guest bedrooms. It is covered in a child's handwriting repeating, "The north star is not fixed."

She has not shown it to Burke because she is afraid he will take it away and tell her she imagined its importance.

- Élise lies to Burke to give the characters ten minutes alone in a room.
- Élise asks the characters to check on a student who has stopped sleeping.
