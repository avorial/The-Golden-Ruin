# Camille Roussel

<div class="wiki-layout">
<div class="wiki-main">

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>At The Table</h2>
<p>Camille Roussel is a forty-six-year-old critic with an exact memory for exhibitions, reputations, and the awkward distance between an artist's ambitions and abilities. Roussel speaks in measured judgments rather than declarations and has ended more than one fashionable argument by recalling the date, venue, and wording of an old review.</p>
<p>At [[Hôtel Bonnet]], Roussel recognized a serious contradiction. The hidden sculpture of [[Janine Du Bois]] was supposedly made ten years ago, but [[Dr. Georges Bonnet]] did not then possess the command required to create it. The work is not merely better than his documented sculpture of that period. It belongs to a mature hand he had not yet become.</p>
</section>

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>Critical Authority</h2>
<p>Roussel writes for respected journals, contributes catalogue essays, advises collectors, and is consulted when attribution becomes contentious. The critic is severe but not needlessly cruel. Honest failure interests Roussel more than fashionable success; fraud inspires lasting anger.</p>
<p>Years of reviewing exhibitions have produced a private archive of catalogues, invitations, clippings, notes, correspondence, and photographs. Roussel's apartment resembles a paper barricade against faulty memory.</p>
</section>

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>The Impossible Sculpture</h2>
<p>Roussel remembers Bonnet's work from a decade earlier: intelligent, ambitious, anatomically informed, and not yet masterful. The Janine sculpture displays confident simplification, controlled surface, and emotional restraint that Bonnet acquired only much later.</p>
<p>Possible explanations include a false date, another sculptor's hand, extensive later reworking, or deliberate fraud. Yet the aging of interior material, old references to the sculpture, and Bonnet's proprietary response make each ordinary answer less satisfactory.</p>
</section>

## Character Block

**Investigative specialties:** Art History, Research, Assess Honesty, Bureaucracy, Evidence Collection, Reputation.

**Core clue:** The Janine sculpture's accepted date conflicts with Bonnet's documented artistic development. Either its history or Bonnet's history is false.

**Leverage:** Provide provenance, a dated photograph, an exhibition catalogue, or permission to examine the sculpture closely.

**Obstacle:** Roussel will distinguish suspicion from proof even when that caution frustrates the investigators.

## The Documentary Witness

Roussel's archive contains enough material to demonstrate that Bonnet's artistic development is impossible. Reviews from different decades describe abrupt losses and recoveries of ability, as though Bonnet repeatedly concealed mastery and then relearned his own public style.

The Janine sculpture may contain older core work beneath a later surface. If Mercier examines the object while Roussel supplies dated descriptions and Janine confirms the sittings, the three witnesses can establish that ordinary provenance cannot explain it.

Roussel is difficult to frighten with threats to reputation but highly vulnerable to destruction of evidence. Once Bonnet realizes the archive matters, papers vanish, a cabinet is forced, or a small fire begins among the catalogues. Saving even part of the collection gives the investigators a documentary path through Bonnet's false lives.

:::
