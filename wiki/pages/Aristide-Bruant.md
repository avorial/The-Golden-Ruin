# Aristide Bruant

<div class="wiki-layout">
<div class="wiki-main">

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>At The Table</h2>
<p>Aristide Bruant insults the rich so well they pay for the privilege. In his cabaret kingdom he is singer, master of ceremonies, judge, gossip, and executioner, with one eyebrow raised over the moral collapse of everyone in the room.</p>
</section>

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>History</h2>
<p>By 1895 Bruant is inseparable from Montmartre's staged decadence: black hat, red scarf, black cape, and a voice that turns gutter speech into performance. His cabaret gives him access to criminals, artists, aristocrats slumming for danger, and performers who know which respectable men are not respectable after midnight.</p>
</section>

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>Campaign Use</h2>
<p>Bruant is a rumor engine. He can send the characters to the right café, warn them away from the wrong patron, or weaponize a song against someone who deserves public humiliation.</p>
</section>
<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>Sourcebook Profile</h2>
<ul>
<li>Singer, master of ceremonies, impresario, and insult comic, he bills himself as the Poet of the Gutter.</li>
<li>Toulouse-Lautrec’s posters immortalize his black slouch hat, red scarf, black cape, slim frame, and hawk-like profile.</li>
<li>At his Montmartre cabaret Le Mirliton he packages demimonde decadence for wealthy patrons who delight in his vulgar abuse.</li>
<li>His arched brows and command of a room allow him to move between performance, intimidation, and confidence.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Published Scenario Uses</h3>
<ul>
<li>As a patron he knows rumors from every social level, regards decadence as something for art rather than cosmic infection, and directs students against the play’s influence.</li>
</ul>
</section>

## Hidden Truth

Bruant has heard enough about the play and the book to know this is not ordinary decadence. He considers Carcosa an invader trespassing on his territory: art may rot, but it should rot honestly.

## GM Ideas

- Bruant sends the characters after a missing cabaret performer last seen reciting lines from an unknown play.
- A new verse appears in one of his songs, and he swears he did not write it.
- His contacts know which patrons of [[House of the North Star]] visit Montmartre under false names.
:::
