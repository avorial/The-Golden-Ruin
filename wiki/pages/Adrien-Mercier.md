# Adrien Mercier

<div class="wiki-layout">
<div class="wiki-main">

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>At The Table</h2>
<p>Adrien Mercier is a thirty-eight-year-old sculptor with the powerful hands, damaged cuffs, and patient concentration of a man who spends more time looking at surfaces than faces. He speaks sparingly until technique is discussed, at which point reserve gives way to exact and occasionally merciless analysis.</p>
<p>Mercier attended [[Dr. Georges Bonnet|Georges Bonnet's]] unveiling at [[Hôtel Bonnet]]. While other guests judged beauty, sentiment, or reputation, Mercier studied tool marks, armature choices, modeling sequence, and the treatment of weight. What he saw disturbed him: Bonnet worked as though trained by masters from generations earlier.</p>
</section>

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>Career and Reputation</h2>
<p>Mercier produces architectural figures, memorial sculpture, portrait busts, and occasional salon pieces. He is respected by foundry workers, stone carvers, and serious sculptors more than by fashionable critics. His work is controlled, anatomically convincing, and accused of lacking poetry.</p>
<p>He accepts that criticism. A monument that remains upright for fifty years matters more to him than a paragraph written after champagne.</p>
</section>

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>What He Saw</h2>
<p>Bonnet's visible work combines modern finish with habits Mercier knows from old manuals, restored monuments, and pieces made before either man was born. The order of operations is wrong for a contemporary studio. Certain joins are concealed in obsolete ways. Tool marks imitate neither fashion nor affectation; they are the unconscious economy of long practice.</p>
<p>Mercier cannot explain this. His first theory is that Bonnet trained secretly under a very old provincial master. His second is that several generations of work have been falsely attributed to one man.</p>
</section>

## Character Block

**Investigative specialties:** Sculpture, Art History, Craft, Architecture, Materials, Forgery.

**Core clue:** Bonnet's methods are not merely old-fashioned. They show ingrained working habits belonging to an earlier century.

**Leverage:** Bring Mercier a photograph, cast, fragment, or authenticated early Bonnet work. He cannot resist examining physical evidence.

**Obstacle:** He refuses claims of sorcery or immortality until the material record eliminates fraud, inherited technique, and misattribution.

## The Technical Witness

Mercier is correct. Bonnet's technique reflects uninterrupted practice across decades, not historical study. With access to the hidden Janine sculpture or authenticated older pieces, Mercier can establish that the same hand made works separated by an impossible span of time.

He is most useful when the investigation needs material confirmation rather than another rumor. He can also identify the concealed hoist, unusual drains, chemical damage, or reinforced floor of Bonnet's atelier as features serving more than ordinary sculpture.

Mercier is not initially brave. Publicly accusing Bonnet could destroy his commissions and make him appear consumed by professional jealousy. Once Nell's murder turns the question from rivalry to evidence, he will provide a signed technical opinion.

:::
