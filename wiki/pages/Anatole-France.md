# Anatole France

<div class="wiki-layout">
<div class="wiki-main">

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>At The Table</h2>
<p>Anatole France is dry, polished, and quietly delighted by human foolishness. He seems to debunk the supernatural because it amuses him, but his skepticism has the discipline of a man holding a door closed with his shoulder.</p>
</section>

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>History</h2>
<p>By 1895 France is known as a novelist, journalist, and essayist with a taste for irony and a cultivated contempt for occult pretension. His recent fiction toys with belief and fraud, and his political conscience will soon draw him toward the defense of Alfred Dreyfus.</p>
</section>

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>Campaign Use</h2>
<p>France can frustrate investigators by explaining away events they know are real. Alternatively, he can become a patron who insists that disbelief is sometimes the only responsible form of containment.</p>
</section>

## Scene Use

| --- | --- |
| describe the impossible | offers three mundane explanations and watches which one fails. |
| ask for help publicly | mocks them to protect them from attention. |
| bring proof of the Yellow Sign | becomes very still and asks who else has seen it. |
| need a literary contact | gives a name with a warning attached. |

:::gm
## Hidden Truth

France may already know that the Yellow Sign spreads through fascination. His public debunking campaign is not ignorance but quarantine.

## GM Ideas

- France hires the characters to discredit a true occult phenomenon before newspapers amplify it.
- His notes include a list of salons where jokes about the King suddenly stopped being jokes.
- He can identify a forged mystical tract because it lacks the vanity of a real occultist.
:::
