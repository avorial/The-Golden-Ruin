# Silas Burke, Head Butler

<div class="wiki-layout">
<div class="wiki-main">

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>At The Table</h2>
<p>Silas Burke is the man who appears before anyone has quite decided to ring. He is broad-shouldered, severe without being theatrical, and dressed with the immaculate restraint of a servant who knows the household's dignity depends on him more than on its owner.</p>

<p>He does not hurry. He does not raise his voice. When students panic, patrons complain, or a visitor tries to force a door, Burke lets the other person spend themselves against his calm.</p>
</section>

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>Place In The House</h2>
<p>Burke served the Vauclair family before Armand de Vauclair's death. He remembers the house when it was still simply a private residence, remembers Cecile de Vauclair in the first raw weeks of grief, and remembers Lucien before the North Star became a retreat for gifted and troublesome children.</p>

<p>That long service gives him a quiet authority no new servant could imitate. He keeps the keys, receives visitors, assigns rooms, watches the service passages, and knows which students require a locked window after dark.</p>
</section>

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>Public Face</h2>
<p>To patrons, Burke is exactly what the North Star promises: discretion made flesh. He knows titles, coats, dietary peculiarities, scandalous relatives, and which name must never be spoken in front of which guest.</p>

<p>To the students, he can seem cold. He is not cruel, but he is very difficult to move. A sobbing child in the corridor receives a blanket, warm milk, and a chair beside the fire. They do not receive answers unless Burke has decided answers will keep them safer than silence.</p>
</section>

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>What He Knows</h2>
<p>Burke knows the true traffic of the house: who leaves their room at night, who receives letters without return addresses, who sketches the North Star without meaning to, and which guests ask too many questions about Armand.</p>

<p>He will share practical information if it protects the household. He will not volunteer family secrets, occult matters, or anything that might turn the North Star into a public curiosity.</p>
</section>

## Hidden Truth

Silas Burke belongs to a quiet mystical society called **the Caretakers**. Its members take positions as butlers, housekeepers, cooks, porters, valets, governesses, and maids in homes where wealth, old blood, art, grief, or strange architecture might draw dark forces close to the world.

The Caretakers do not rule these houses. They watch them. They keep fires burning in the correct rooms, remove books before the wrong guest opens them, stand outside nursery doors at impossible hours, and make certain that people who should not meet are delayed by coats, cabs, meals, or missing keys.

Burke came into Vauclair service before Armand's death because the society already suspected the North Star property was dangerous. Armand's suicide confirmed that something had found purchase in the house, but not what it was.

Burke's loyalty is therefore divided:

- He respects Lucien and helped preserve the family after Armand died.
- He believes the students are safer inside the North Star than scattered among careless families.
- He will sacrifice reputation, employment, and even one life if he believes it prevents a larger breach.

## What Burke Is Hiding

Burke has copies of keys that Lucien does not know exist. One opens a sealed room connected to Armand's final weeks. Another fits a servants' door that should not exist on the floor plan.

He keeps a Caretaker mark behind the watch in his waistcoat: a small silver charm shaped like a closed hand holding a star.

He has sent at least three letters to other Caretakers since the first American students arrived.

## Pressure Moves

- Burke quietly changes room assignments to keep vulnerable students away from certain windows.
- Burke intercepts a letter and returns it opened but resealed.
- Burke refuses Lucien an order in private, then resumes perfect service in public.
- Burke asks a character to trust him without explaining why.
