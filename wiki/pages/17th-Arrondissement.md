# 17th Arrondissement

## Public Face
In the 1890s, Paris’s 17th arrondissement was a district of sharp contrasts. Around Plaine-de-Monceaux, Ternes, and Avenue de Wagram, broad avenues, new apartment houses, private mansions, fashionable restaurants, and respectable bourgeois households gave the area an air of wealth and order. Artists, financiers, professionals, foreign visitors, and old families mixed in salons, cafés, theatres, and dining rooms, while Parc Monceau and the great avenues toward the Étoile made the western part of the arrondissement feel closely connected to fashionable Paris.

Farther north and east, toward Batignolles and Place de Clichy, the atmosphere became livelier and less formal. Cafés, cabarets, artists’ haunts, working households, theatres, and cheaper restaurants stood only a short distance from expensive homes. That mixture made the 17th especially useful for people who wanted to move between respectable society and bohemian Paris without crossing the entire city. In 1890, it could feel polished, artistic, discreet, and slightly dangerous, depending on which street one turned down.
