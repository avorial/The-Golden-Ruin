# Micar

## Overview

Medieval gargoyles imitate the Micar, not the reverse. Its glossy black skin resembles carved obsidian, encouraging witnesses to mistake a living predator for architectural stone.

## Investigation and Response

- A supposed gargoyle changes rooflines between sightings.
- Scratches and bites begin turning flesh hard and crystalline.
- When washed in blood, a dead Micar's hide reflects scenes from the past.
- Living skin or storm clouds may show fragments of a catastrophic future.

The creature may serve a Carcosan noble and becomes more dangerous when investigators dismiss it as an animated statue. Its hide is valuable evidence and a dangerous occult instrument.
