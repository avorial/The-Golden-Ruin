# Théâtre Lyrique

## Overview

The Haussmann-era building burned during the Commune and was rebuilt to the same design in 1874. Spoken dialogue defines opéra-comique, not humor; Sarah Bernhardt will later take over the venue.

## Historical Background

The Théâtre Lyrique (French pronunciation: [teɑtʁ liʁik]) was one of four opera companies performing in Paris during the middle of the 19th century (the other three being the Opéra, the Opéra-Comique, and the Théâtre-Italien). The company was founded in 1847 as the Opéra-National by the French composer Adolphe Adam and renamed Théâtre Lyrique in 1852. It used four different theatres in succession, the Cirque Olympique, the Théâtre Historique, the Salle du Théâtre-Lyrique (now the Théâtre de la Ville), and the Salle de l'Athénée, until it ceased operations in 1872.
