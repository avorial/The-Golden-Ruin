# Madame Eugénie Voclain, Head Cook

<div class="wiki-layout">
<div class="wiki-main">

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>At The Table</h2>
<p>Madame Voclain rules the kitchen as if it were a principality under siege. She is stout, practical, sharp-eyed, and capable of reducing a visiting aristocrat to silence with one look at the untouched food on his plate.</p>

<p>She believes in butter, broth, sleep, hot wine when appropriate, and the moral failure of wasting good bread. She has no patience for melodrama, which makes her surprisingly useful when melodrama becomes dangerous.</p>
</section>

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>Place In The House</h2>
<p>Every secret in the North Star eventually passes through the kitchen. Students who refuse breakfast, patrons who demand private trays, servants who whisper near the pantry, and guests who require laudanum in their tea all leave traces in Madame Voclain's ledgers.</p>

<p>She feeds the household well because hunger makes frightened people foolish. Her menus are also a map of the house's condition. Rich soups follow bad nights. Plain meals appear when she suspects the household has grown too indulgent. Sugared pears arrive when a child has been crying.</p>
</section>

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>Public Face</h2>
<p>To guests, Madame Voclain is rarely seen and frequently praised. To students, she is a gruff source of rolls, chocolate, scoldings, and practical advice.</p>

<p>She is not sentimental in speech. Her kindness arrives on plates, in covered bowls, or as a servant sent upstairs with a hot brick wrapped in flannel.</p>
</section>

## Hidden Truth

Madame Voclain is not formally one of the Caretakers. She knows they exist, calls them "Burke's solemn little brotherhood," and thinks mystical societies would do better work if they ate at regular hours.

That skepticism does not make her ignorant. Burke relies on her because she notices bodily truths: exhaustion, poisoning, obsession, pregnancy, terror, and the peculiar hollow appetite of someone who has been touched by Carcosa.

## What Voclain Is Hiding

She has kept her own ledger since Armand's final months. It records meals, visitors, illnesses, and late trays sent to locked rooms. Several entries contradict the official account of the days before Armand died.

She keeps the ledger wrapped in oilcloth beneath a loose tile in the pantry.

## Pressure Moves

- Voclain refuses to serve a guest she believes is dangerous, forcing Lucien or Burke to explain the insult.
- Voclain sends a student to the characters with food and a warning.
- Voclain reveals one page from her ledger, then demands the characters earn the rest.
