# Théo Marchand

<div class="wiki-layout">
<div class="wiki-main">

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>At The Table</h2>
<p>Théo Marchand is a thirty-five-year-old painter, dinner guest, and collector of other people's inconsistencies. He is charming enough to be invited, amusing enough to remain, and observant enough to leave with more than the host intended to reveal.</p>
<p>At Bonnet's unveiling, Marchand repeated rumors about gaps in the sculptor's history: patrons who remembered an identical relative, old work appearing under uncertain names, and stories Bonnet told differently depending on his audience.</p>
</section>

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>The Painter</h2>
<p>Marchand paints competent portraits and fashionable interiors. His pictures sell because sitters recognize themselves at their most flattering. Serious critics call him facile. Théo answers that they still spell his name correctly.</p>
<p>His real talent is social composition. He knows where people stand, who watches whom, which guest has been omitted, and what subject empties a room. He treats a salon like a canvas whose figures rearrange themselves whenever a secret enters.</p>
</section>

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>A Useful Gossip</h2>
<p>Marchand does not repeat everything. Indiscriminate gossip stops receiving invitations. He trades information for better information, access, favors, introductions, and the pleasure of being first to understand a scandal.</p>
<p>He remembers Bonnet stories from critics, models, dealers, framers, photographers, and older artists. Individually they sound petty. Together they form a biography that refuses to proceed in chronological order.</p>
</section>

## Character Block

**Investigative specialties:** Gossip, Flattery, Art History, Assess Honesty, Reassurance, Streetwise.

**Core clue:** Bonnet's public biography changes with the listener, while stories of an identical predecessor recur across several artistic generations.

**Leverage:** Offer Marchand a genuine secret, an exclusive introduction, or proof that a rumor is larger than adultery and professional fraud.

**Obstacle:** If treated as a fool or mere gossip, he retaliates by letting an embarrassing truth reach the least convenient ears.

## What Marchand Has Heard

Marchand can connect investigators to three useful rumors: an elderly patron who remembers “Bonnet's father” with the same face, a dealer who handled unsigned sculpture in Bonnet's unmistakable style decades too early, and a photographer whom Bonnet paid to destroy a plate.

He initially believes the explanation is fraud, inheritance, or a family secret. Proof of longevity frightens him because it converts every entertaining contradiction into evidence that Bonnet may have killed to protect.

Marchand survives by knowing when to talk. Nell's death teaches him that he may now need to know when to remain silent. If protected and treated seriously, he becomes an excellent recurring contact across galleries, cafés, theatres, newspapers, and private salons.

:::
