# Pierre-Auguste Renoir

<div class="wiki-layout">
<div class="wiki-main">

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>At The Table</h2>
<p>Renoir prefers warmth, flesh, sunlight, and ordinary happiness. He has little patience for theatrical dread and less interest in being dragged into someone else's occult crisis.</p>
</section>

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>History</h2>
<p>Born the son of a tailor, Renoir survived poverty and critical scorn to become one of the central Impressionists. By 1895 he enjoys the respectability he once lacked, and he does not intend to trade it for fashionable madness.</p>
</section>

<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>Campaign Use</h2>
<p>Renoir is useful precisely because he resists the weird. If he admits something is wrong, the characters should take it seriously.</p>
</section>
<section class="wiki-section">
<h2>Sourcebook Profile</h2>
<ul>
<li>The tailor’s son endured poverty and ridicule before gaining respect as a leading Impressionist.</li>
<li>Appeals to hard-won respectability are the best way to secure his cooperation.</li>
<li>The serenity and beauty of his pictures mirror a comparatively calm private life.</li>
<li>He has no interest in occultism, strangeness, or adventure and resists attempts to draw him into any of them.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Published Scenario Uses</h3>
<ul>
<li>Use him as a grounded artistic authority whose disbelief forces investigators to present a practical, respectable reason for involving him.</li>
</ul>
</section>

## Hidden Truth

Renoir has noticed that a certain collector only buys joyful scenes if they contain one child looking away from the viewer. He thinks it is a bad taste. It may be a selection rule.

## GM Ideas

- Renoir points the characters toward a respectable collection hiding a Carcosan canvas.
- A painting of a picnic develops a black lake in the background.
- His refusal to engage makes him a good measure of when events have become undeniable.
:::
